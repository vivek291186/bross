Playbook for next week's songs

  1. Major key, 4/4, 110-125 BPM for dance songs; keep one slow 60-70 BPM wind-down song in the week.
  2. One 1-2 bar hook repeated in every chorus, simple melody inside about 5 notes; add nonsense syllables (la la, doo doo) kids can sing before they can talk.
  3. Every lyric line has an action kids can copy (Total Physical Response): clap, jump, wiggle, freeze, stomp.
  4. Use call-and-response or riddle lines ('What colour? RED!') with a short pause so kids can answer at home.
  5. A tiny story with one tempo change kids can act out (speed up, slow down, freeze), ending with a happy payoff.
  6. A child's voice as the lead singer; characters look into the camera and talk to the viewer.
  7. Subjects: family, animals, dogs, dinosaurs, vehicles, helpers, pretend play jobs and daily routines; each week make 2 songs as fresh Bro Bros twists on public domain rhymes (Wheels on the Bus, Old MacDonald, Five Little Ducks) with an animal or job twist and a learning tag in the title.
  8. Half step key lift for the final chorus; start each song with a 2 bar clap or drum pattern (rhythm hooks babies first) and colour the arrangement with bright high register sounds (bells, xylophone, ukulele).
  9. Camera and motion: calm cuts every 3 to 6 s, lead Bro centred and big, gentle constant background motion (drifting clouds, bubbles, stars) that matches the song, bright rounded visuals, big friendly eyes; no flashing, no sudden loud sounds.
  10. Hide one learning point (counting, colours, routine, feelings, safety) and name it in the description; at least one song a week should be a healthy habit or safety song parents search for (dentist, seatbelt, crossing the road, manners).
  11. Shorts and Reels: our skip rate in the first 3 s is 58 to 90%, so open on full body dancing at the chorus peak or a direct action call (wave, jump, race), never on slow setup; make each Short a new cut with its own hook and loop back to the start.
  12. Thumbnails: one happy face, one clear action, one recognisable object, big eyes, max 3 words, bright plain background; never scared, crying or shocked faces; test changes by hand because Test and Compare is off for kids videos.
  13. Weekly long form: one 10 to 20 minute compilation (5 or 6 songs, best performer first) every Friday, the strongest kids upload day; keep a 24/7 live stream of all songs running and point stream playlists back to new uploads.
  14. Languages: add re-sung audio tracks (not low quality AI voice swaps) to the best songs in Spanish, Portuguese, Indonesian and Arabic first, then Hindi; translate titles and descriptions of the top songs into 20 languages to test demand; keep one channel with multi audio rather than new language channels.
  15. Originality and compliance: write our own lyrics, vary story situations and endings week to week (no template clones), tick the AI music disclosure on every upload, set made for kids per video, and make every Suno song on the paid plan.
  16. Mix in older kid energy: at least two songs a week should be full body dance alongs (Danny Go! style) with a funny chantable nonsense word kids can shout; group Shorts of the same song family into a YouTube Shorts series and, once we pass 1,000 Instagram followers, test two hooks per song with Trial Reels.
  17. Titles: story hook plus an age keyword (for toddlers, learn numbers 1-10, bedtime for preschoolers), max 2 caps words and 1 to 2 emojis, no fear, fight, medical or danger words; put every video in a keyword playlist and link the playlist's first video in the description.
  18. Seasonal plan: until 24 Dec post at least 3 holiday neutral winter or lights songs a week (snow, cookies, twinkle lights), each with a sunny summer variant for the southern hemisphere; start Ramadan and Lunar New Year friendly songs in January.

YouTube algorithm · 26
  • YouTube's recommender has two goals: help each viewer find what they want and maximise long-term satisfaction; in 2026 satisfaction (surveys, rewatches, 'not interested') outweighs raw watch time. (YouTube blog via SocialPilot / Shopify, 2026, high)
  • Recommendations are not one system: Home, Suggested, Search, Shorts feed and Subscriptions each rank with different signals; Suggested looks mainly at the video being watched now. (posteverywhere / subsub.io, Oct 2026, high)
  • Long-form: click-through rate on thumbnail+title and average view duration work as a pair; the thumbnail must deliver what it promises or satisfaction drops. (blckalpaca / vidIQ, 2026, high)
  • Shorts run on a separate engine: each Short goes to a seed audience (a few hundred viewers); if they watch instead of swiping away, reach widens in waves. (vidIQ / OutlierKit, 2026, high)
  • Shorts key signals: viewed vs swiped away, watch-through %, loops/replays and shares; benchmark about 65% average view duration for Shorts under 30 s, about 50% for 30-60 s. (socialync / dataslayer, 2026, medium)
  • The first 1.5 to 3 seconds of a Short decide most swipe-aways, so the opening frame must already be the hook. (easyviral / clipspeed, 2026, medium)
  • Since 1 Oct 2026 YouTube's recommender gives more reach to original Shorts; reused or inauthentic content is reviewed at channel level. (AIR Media-Tech (YouTube partner), Oct 2026, high)
  • Views now count from the first frame (Shorts since Mar 2025, all formats since Aug 2026), so raw views look bigger; engaged views and retention are the real health metrics. (SocialPilot, Sep 2026, high)
  • Made-for-kids videos lose comments, notification bell, cards/end screens and personalised ads, so discovery relies even more on watch behaviour, Search and Suggested. (AIR glossary / YouTube Help, high)
  • YouTube Kids app recommendations come from what each child profile watched and searched; homepage shelves are series, music, learning, discovery and games. (YouTube Kids Help / ppc.land, high)
  • New channels typically get cautious distribution for the first few months until the system has performance history; consistency builds that trust. (FluxNote, Mar 2026, medium)
  • Instagram Reels rank on watch time first, then sends (DM shares) per reach, then likes per reach; sends matter most for reaching non-followers; reposts and watermarks are demoted. (SocialPilot Aug 2026 / Later, high)
  • For kids songs, compilations and 'Up Next' sessions matter: if the next suggested video is yours, you keep the session; stitch 5-6 songs into mega mixes led by the best performer. (nursery rhyme growth analyses, medium)
  • Because made for kids videos lose end screens, cards and the bell, keyword named playlists are the main discovery tool; descriptions should link to the first video of a related playlist so autoplay continues in order. (AIR Media-Tech, Oct 2026, medium)
  • For kids and teens long form, Friday is the best upload day and Wednesday the weakest, a +379% gap (the biggest of any niche); peak slots are 16:00 UTC and 23:00 UTC, and 10 to 20 minute videos fit best. (AIR study of 31,852 videos, Jul 2026, medium)
  • 24/7 live streams of the back catalogue gave a kids channel 41.9% of its watch time from under a fifth of its views, at 66% higher RPM; big kids channels run 4 to 5 streams at once like mini TV stations. (AIR case, Mar 2026, medium)
  • A kids stream view earned about 1.45 USD per 1,000 versus 0.43 for a normal video view; on one 8M channel streams were only 4% of views while total channel views grew 53%. (AIR, Oct 2026, medium)
  • In a 282 channel study, large kids channels earned the most total revenue at 16 or more videos a month, so our 14 a week cadence is in the winning range. (AIR, Oct 2026, medium)
  • Once a channel is in the YouTube Kids app it can become the main source: YouTube Kids brought 67% of traffic for Baby Ben en Espanol and 68 to 78% for the BIBO toy channels. (AIR, Oct 2026, medium)
  • Since Jan 2026 parents can cap the Shorts feed for kids and teens, even to zero, so Shorts cannot be the only growth engine; long form and playlists must carry the channel. (AIR / YouTube, Oct 2026, medium)
  • YouTube keeps autoplay off by default and Take a Break and Bedtime reminders on for signed in under 18s, so continuation to our next video cannot be assumed; long compilations and playlists inside one session matter more. (YouTube blog, Jan 2026, medium)
  • YouTube's Todd Beaupre (Sep 2026): a new upload's first hour is not a subscriber test; it is shown to mixed audiences including people who never watched the channel, and each audience is judged separately. (Creator Insider via PPC Land, Sep 2026, high)
  • Even a channel's best videos are usually picked by only about 10% or fewer of subscribers who see them in the feed, so low subscriber click rate is normal and not a penalty. (Creator Insider via PPC Land, Sep 2026, high)

Music that attracts kids · 28
  • Upbeat tempo drives movement; Baby Shark sits at about 117 BPM. Aim for 110-125 BPM for dance songs. (Business Today / Daily Beast experts, high)
  • Heavy repetition of a 1-2 bar melodic hook and of whole sections is the common thread of mega-hits. (BMI analysis, high)
  • Narrow, simple melody (about 5 notes) lets small kids sing along, which rewards them and drives replays. (music analysis articles, medium)
  • Family words (baby, mommy, daddy, grandma) create an emotional bond with the song. (Salimpoor (neuroscience), Daily Beast, high)
  • Story moments with tempo change (speed up for 'run away', slow for 'safe at last') let kids act the song out. (Ilari (USC), Daily Beast, high)
  • A key change up a half-step near the end lifts energy without making it harder to sing. (BMI analysis, medium)
  • Cocomelon uses song duos (call and answer parts) and reuses the same melodies with simple lyrics. (Cocomelon case study PDF, medium)
  • Children of all ages in studies prefer major-key songs over minor-key songs, and familiarity with a song strongly raises how much they like it. (Dobrota & Gusic study, J. Elementary Education, high)
  • Preschoolers (5-6) like many styles but rate a children's song sung by a child highest; a kid lead voice beats an adult voice. (Habe & Bratina study of 250 preschoolers, high)
  • Kids aged 4-6 in the US and Poland prefer songs they already know with a fast tempo and a strong beat; they react by moving and drawing rather than describing. (Vasil & Kondracka-Szala, J. Popular Music Education 2025, high)
  • Over 80% of popular and kids music is in major keys and 2/4 or 4/4 time; staying there keeps songs instantly singable. (music preference research summary, medium)
  • Super Simple Songs builds songs on Total Physical Response: every lyric pairs with an action, tempo slowed so toddlers can act each word, with short pauses for kids to echo. (Super Simple about page / SSS analyses, high)
  • Call-and-response (riddle style 'I see something...?' or echo lines) gives kids a safe place to join in and a small reward when they answer. (Super Simple Songs analyses, medium)
  • Calm songs for wind-down sit around 60-70 BPM (resting heart rate); keep a bedtime/lullaby lane alongside 110-125 BPM dance songs. (SSS Little Snowflake analysis, medium)
  • Nonsense syllables like 'doo doo doo' make songs singable for pre-verbal toddlers; Baby Shark's family call-and-response is the model. (BabyYumYum / Baby Shark analyses, medium)
  • Babies recognise music by 3 months, but only by 12 months do they move differently to real songs than to scrambled ones, mostly with the upper body, and none keep the beat; simple bounce and arm moves suit the youngest viewers. (eLife study via Earth.com, Jul 2026, high)
  • Higher pitched music drew a stronger brain response at 6 months and slightly more movement at every age tested; bright high register sounds (bells, glockenspiel, xylophone, kid voices) grab babies. (eLife study via Earth.com, Jul 2026, high)
  • Newborns already anticipate a steady beat while melody is learned slowly, so a strong clap or drum pattern is the first hook for the youngest; the melody hook comes second. (HealthDay, Feb 2026, medium)
  • A 16 country study of over 2,000 babies found they listen longer to sing song baby talk (slower, melodic, simple words) than to adult speech; spoken lines in songs should use that sing song delivery. (UChicago Chatter Lab / ManyBabies, Jul 2026, high)
  • From about 10 months many babies prefer the voices of other young children, which backs a kid lead singer; but preferences vary by culture, so no single voice style is universal. (UChicago Chatter Lab, Jul 2026, medium)
  • Pinkfong's Bebefinn runs on near constant looping songs in short segments that parents use as a 'one more song' timer, with a sibling trio and supportive parents. (Screenwise, 2026, medium)
  • Veteran kids songwriter Laurie Berkner builds audience participation and movement into the song itself and avoids talking down to kids; songs that let kids move when told to sit still are the ones they love. (My Weekly Mixtape, Jun 2026, medium)
  • An 8 week rhythm and movement program (clapping, tapping sticks, body percussion in time with others) gave 200+ four year olds better social skills and fewer behaviour problems; clap along parts make a song valued by parents and teachers. (Behavioral Sciences via EurekAlert, Apr 2026, high)
  • Danny Go! builds its catalogue as dance games: freeze dances, fast versus slow animal dances, pretend danger games ('sharks in the water'), 'hyperspeed' remixes and long brain break compilations used in classrooms. (Danny Go! video titles / parent reviews, 2026, medium)
  • Parents say kids love songs about their favourite things (garbage trucks, diggers, bugs, trains) and useful songs like a clean up tune that actually gets them tidying. (New Orleans Mom, 2026, medium)
  • CoComelon treats music as the main teaching tool: episodes open with a song and transitions are sung; since 2026 learning experts from UCLA's Center for Scholars and Storytellers shape its season goals and scripts. (The Signal, Apr 2026, high)
  • Babies who were sung to often by 6 months had larger vocabularies at 14 months, and a 2025 review of six studies found songs help word learning and speech fluency. (J. Child Language / review via The Signal, Apr 2026, high)
  • Young children's ability to detect rhythm and patterns is linked to grammar and early reading skills, so steady rhymes and predictable line patterns help learning as well as catchiness. (The Signal, Apr 2026, medium)
