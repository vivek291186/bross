Playbook for next week's songs

  1. Keep dance songs at 110-125 BPM with a 1-2 bar hook repeated in every chorus.
  2. Build each song around a tiny story with one tempo change kids can act out (speed up / slow down / freeze).
  3. Use family or animal characters and call-and-answer lines (song duos).
  4. Add a half-step key lift for the final chorus.
  5. Keep cuts calm (3-6 s), avoid flashing lights and sudden loud sounds; bright rounded visuals.
  6. Hide a learning point in each song (counting, colours, routine, safety) and say it in the description.
  7. Plan dubbed versions of the best songs for the biggest kids markets (Spanish, Portuguese, Hindi, Indonesian, Arabic); multi-language audio is the biggest proven growth lever.
  8. Make long compilations of the week's songs for evergreen views.
  9. Keep everything original and clearly not mass-produced to stay inside YouTube's quality principles.
  10. Open every Short and Reel on the most exciting moment (the hook line plus big dance move) in the first second; no title cards first.
  11. Make Shorts loop: end on a frame that flows back into the start, so replays count.
  12. Thumbnail and title must show exactly what the song delivers (no bait) to protect satisfaction signals.

  • The first 1.5 to 3 seconds of a Short decide most swipe-aways, so the opening frame must already be the hook. (easyviral / clipspeed, 2026, medium)
  • Since 1 Oct 2026 YouTube's recommender gives more reach to original Shorts; reused or inauthentic content is reviewed at channel level. (AIR Media-Tech (YouTube partner), Oct 2026, high)
  • Views now count from the first frame (Shorts since Mar 2025, all formats since Aug 2026), so raw views look bigger; engaged views and retention are the real health metrics. (SocialPilot, Sep 2026, high)
  • Made-for-kids videos lose comments, notification bell, cards/end screens and personalised ads, so discovery relies even more on watch behaviour, Search and Suggested. (AIR glossary / YouTube Help, high)
  • YouTube Kids app recommendations come from what each child profile watched and searched; homepage shelves are series, music, learning, discovery and games. (YouTube Kids Help / ppc.land, high)
  • New channels typically get cautious distribution for the first few months until the system has performance history; consistency builds that trust. (FluxNote, Mar 2026, medium)
  • Instagram Reels rank on watch time first, then sends (DM shares) per reach, then likes per reach; sends matter most for reaching non-followers; reposts and watermarks are demoted. (SocialPilot Aug 2026 / Later, high)

Music that attracts kids · 7
  • Upbeat tempo drives movement; Baby Shark sits at about 117 BPM. Aim for 110-125 BPM for dance songs. (Business Today / Daily Beast experts, high)
  • Heavy repetition of a 1-2 bar melodic hook and of whole sections is the common thread of mega-hits. (BMI analysis, high)
  • Narrow, simple melody (about 5 notes) lets small kids sing along, which rewards them and drives replays. (music analysis articles, medium)
  • Family words (baby, mommy, daddy, grandma) create an emotional bond with the song. (Salimpoor (neuroscience), Daily Beast, high)
  • Story moments with tempo change (speed up for 'run away', slow for 'safe at last') let kids act the song out. (Ilari (USC), Daily Beast, high)
  • A key change up a half-step near the end lifts energy without making it harder to sing. (BMI analysis, medium)
  • Cocomelon uses song duos (call and answer parts) and reuses the same melodies with simple lyrics. (Cocomelon case study PDF, medium)
Visuals & animation · 6
  • Kids like to see other kids dancing on screen; the version of Baby Shark with real kids dancing is the one that went viral. (Ilari (USC), Daily Beast, high)
  • Big eyes, round geometric shapes and saturated colours attract young viewers. (Daily Beast, high)
  • YouTube-native kids content is about twice as fast and brighter than broadcast kids TV. (Enders Analysis, Dec 2025, high)
  • Cocomelon cuts every 1-3 s, but experts criticise this as over-stimulating; parents move kids to calmer shows like Bluey. (shapes.inc / screenwise, medium)
  • Pixar-like rounded 3D style is proven with young kids. (Cocomelon case study, medium)
  • Character-led storytelling is beating plain tutorial/fact content in 2026; recurring characters are the long-term asset. (Whizzy Studios 2026, medium)
Topics kids love · 5
  • Animal families, vehicles (bus, car, trucks), and everyday routines (brushing teeth, bath, bedtime) are evergreen hits. (most-viewed lists, Tubefilter, high)
  • Tooth-brushing videos get a seasonal boost around October (Halloween candy). (Tubefilter, Oct 2026, medium)
  • Farm machines and 'satisfying' efficiency videos are popular with kids. (Tubefilter, Jan 2026, medium)
  • Learning in disguise (phonics, numbers, colours inside a fun song) is a top trend; ChuChu's Phonics Song is India's most-viewed video (5.8B). (Wikipedia list / AIR, high)
  • Cute kids plus cute pets push views even higher. (Tubefilter, Apr 2026, medium)

YouTube rules & safety · 5
  • YouTube's quality principles push high-quality made-for-kids videos up in recommendations and decide entry to the YouTube Kids app. (YouTube Help / YouTube blog, high)
  • Six low-quality types to avoid: heavily promotional, negative behaviour, fake-educational, hard to follow/mass-produced, sensational or keyword-stuffed, odd use of familiar characters. (AIR summary of YouTube policy, high)
  • Red flags: flashing lights, loud or sudden sounds, chaotic camera moves, rushed storytelling, mean characters even as jokes. (Whizzy Studios 2026, medium)
  • YouTube is cracking down on low-effort AI 'slop' for kids; originality and consistent quality matter more in 2026. (AIR Jan 2026 / YouTube CEO letter, high)
  • Getting into the YouTube Kids app is done through review (often via an official YouTube partner), not by applying directly. (AIR, medium)
