Playbook for next week's songs

  1. Major key, 4/4, 110-125 BPM for dance songs; keep one slow 60-70 BPM wind-down song in the week.
  2. One 1-2 bar hook repeated in every chorus, simple melody inside about 5 notes sitting mostly between middle C and A (C, F or G major), where preschoolers sing best; add nonsense syllables (la la, doo doo) kids can sing before they can talk.
  3. Every lyric line has an action kids can copy (Total Physical Response): clap, jump, wiggle, freeze, stomp.
  4. Use call-and-response or riddle lines ('What colour? RED!') with a short pause so kids can answer at home.
  5. A tiny story that is easy to follow (one problem, one fix, one clear lesson such as kindness or a healthy habit) with one tempo change kids can act out, ending in a happy payoff; show people and places from many cultures.
  6. A bright, young sounding lead voice (in Suno ask for 'bright youthful vocals' rather than 'children's choir', which often comes out uncanny); characters look into the camera and talk to the viewer, with spoken lines in their own [Spoken] section; master every song to about -14 LUFS with -1 dBTP peaks.
  7. Subjects: family, animals, dogs, dinosaurs, vehicles, helpers, pretend play jobs and daily routines; each week make 2 songs as fresh Bro Bros twists on public domain rhymes (Wheels on the Bus, Old MacDonald, Five Little Ducks) with an animal or job twist and a learning tag in the title.
  8. Half step key lift for the final chorus; start each song with a 2 bar clap or drum pattern (rhythm hooks babies first) and colour the arrangement with bright high register sounds (bells, xylophone, ukulele); reach the chorus by about 0:15, because our long songs hold viewers only about 20 seconds on average.
  9. Camera and motion: calm cuts every 3 to 6 s, lead Bro centred and big, gentle constant background motion (drifting clouds, bubbles, stars) that matches the song, bright rounded visuals, big friendly eyes; no flashing, no sudden loud sounds.
  10. Hide one learning point (counting, colours, routine, feelings, safety) and name it in the description; every week make at least one healthy habit song led by a friendly helper mascot (brush teeth for two minutes, wash hands) and one sharing or taking turns song, the topics behind this autumn's biggest kids breakouts.
  11. Shorts and Reels: our skip rate in the first 3 s is 58 to 90%, so open on full body dancing at the chorus peak, a direct action call or a tiny mystery question ('Who took the cookie?'), with motion or text in the first half second; make each Short a new cut with its own hook that loops back to the start; keep music Shorts about 30 to 40 s and set each Short's related video link to its full song.
  12. Thumbnails: one happy Bro face cropped big (about 40% of the frame height), one clear action, one recognisable object, high contrast with a warm accent on a plain bright background (red and white or blue and orange), max 3 outlined words kept out of the bottom right corner; never scared, crying or shocked faces; test changes by hand because Test and Compare is off for kids videos.
  13. Weekly long form: one 20 to 30 minute themed compilation (one topic, best performer first, a bonus blooper dance at the end) every Friday; make the 25 Dec and 1 Jan Fridays holiday mega mixes; keep a 24/7 live stream of all songs running once we pass about 50 songs.
  14. Languages: add re-sung audio tracks (not low quality AI voice swaps) to the best songs in Spanish, Portuguese, Indonesian and Arabic first, then Hindi; translate titles and descriptions of the top songs into 20 languages to test demand; keep one channel with multi audio rather than new language channels.
  15. Originality and compliance: write our own lyrics, vary story situations and endings week to week (no template clones), tick the AI music disclosure on every upload, set made for kids per video, and make every Suno song on the paid plan.
  16. Mix in older kid energy: at least two songs a week should be full body dance alongs (Danny Go! style) with a funny chantable nonsense word kids can shout; on Instagram post fewer, better Reels (the best 1 or 2 songs a day), captions as one keyword sentence plus a question to parents and at most 2 hashtags; group Shorts of one song family into a YouTube Shorts series and test two hooks per song with Trial Reels after 1,000 followers.
  17. Titles: Suggested videos bring two thirds of our views, so use the same plain topic words as the big channels' hits (bus, doctor, brush teeth, dinosaur) plus a story hook and an age keyword, max 2 caps words and 1 to 2 emojis, no fear, fight, medical or danger words; put every video in a keyword playlist and link the playlist's first video in the description.
  18. Seasonal plan: until 24 Dec post at least 3 holiday neutral winter or lights songs a week (snow, cookies, twinkle lights), each with a sunny summer variant for the southern hemisphere; post festival songs 2 to 3 days before the day (our Diwali songs doubled daily views) and holiday Shorts within the 4 weeks before; have holiday compilations live by 1 Dec when ad rates peak; keep star and gift songs going to Three Kings Day (6 Jan) for Latin America and the Philippines; start Ramadan and Lunar New Year friendly songs in January.
